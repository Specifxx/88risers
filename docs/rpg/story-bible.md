# 88 Risers — RPG story bible

An adaptation of the Body Tea / Wukhan lore for an open-world RPG in the Morrowind mould: one hand-made island, no quest markers, factions you join, and NPCs who each run on their own AI personality.

## The premise in one paragraph

The dead cannot rise on their own any more. Anything that dies unattended sinks into the ground, rots and comes back as a monster. A **Riser** is someone who knows the rite that lets the dead rise and pass on instead. There used to be 88 of them. Now there are eight, in one village, inside one small ring of safe ground kept by a broken machine. You arrive as a pilgrim. Your pilgrimage is to bring the number back to **88**, because that is how many it takes to do the machine's job without the machine.

## Cosmology

Three layers: **the Still** above (nothingness, completion, rest), the living world in the middle, and **the Under** below (not punishment, just death that was never finished).

1. The Winged Lion governs the whole cycle. Life descends from its flame; the dead rise through its wings and pass to the Still. Nothing rots.
2. Gornn tears the wings off. Life still comes down. Death no longer goes up. The Lion falls out of the heavens and lands on the island, wingless but still alive, its flame burning low in its chest.
3. Mankind is taught the Rite of Rising: a way to say "Lion, gather this one", one death at a time. Those who say it are the Risers. At their height there are 88, enough to cover the world.
4. The world grows. The 88 are overwhelmed. Instead of teaching more people, they go to the fallen Lion, take the ember from its chest, and build Wukhan around it to say the rite for everyone. They stop doing it themselves. Without its ember the Lion turns to stone. This is their sin: they outsourced the one job that was theirs, and they put out their god to do it.
5. **Gornn comes back.** The dragon who tore off the Lion's wings sees a machine shaped like a monkey doing the sacred work he wanted for dragons, and he comes to destroy it. Wukhan fights him and drives him off, but takes damage he cannot repair.
6. **The Fall.** Wukhan is broken. He can no longer reach the whole world. With what he has left he bends his staff into the Ring and protects the ground inside it, repeating the only order he still understands: *Protect the Ring.* Outside it, the dead sink. Nobody in Rhodes knows what broke him; the fight is remembered only as a great evil that Wukhan defeated.
7. Gornn survived, wounded, somewhere on the island.

### Where the Lion is

Rhodes teaches that the Lion is in the heavens, watching. It is not. It is a stone statue far outside the Ring, in the country of the Undying. The Undying guard it and know exactly what it is: even as stone it gives off a trace of life, and that trace is what rebuilds them when they die. To everyone else who has seen it, it is a very old carving of a lion with broken shoulders.

## Why 88, and what the power is for

Wukhan was built to replace exactly 88 people. So 88 Risers can carry what he carries.

The Ring never changes size. It is Wukhan's staff, and it protects Rhodes and nothing else. But a Riser keeps the ground around *them* safe, wherever they are: deaths near a Riser rise instead of sinking. Recruits stay in their own settlements, so each one becomes a small safe place outside the Ring. One recruit is one safe town. **At 88, there are enough safe places to cover the island, and Wukhan is no longer needed.**

That matters because of what is inside him. The Lion's ember is still caged in his core, and it is the only thing that can wake the stone Lion. It cannot be freed while he is running, and he cannot be switched off while the Ring depends on him: everyone inside would die unrisen. With 88 Risers holding the island, he can finally be stopped and the ember released.

### Why people believe it

- **The first 88 left an instruction.** Before they died out, they carved one line above the Register: *"If he fails, gather the 88."* They meant: take the job back. It is the only thing they left, and it is why the Register has 88 lines.
- **Rhodes reads it the other way.** After generations living under the machine's protection, people revere Wukhan. To them the line means: the 88 built him, so 88 Risers can mend him. The Houses teach this reading, because it keeps Wukhan, and them, at the centre.
- **Wukhan answers them.** When a Riser says the rite near the machine, the ember in his core flares. People take that as Wukhan drawing strength from Risers, and conclude that enough of them would restore him. In truth the ember is answering the rite, as the Lion always did, and Wukhan has nothing to do with it.

So the evidence is real and the conclusion is wrong. Everyone in Rhodes believes the Pilgrimage exists to gather 88 Risers to *repair* Wukhan. The game is the slow discovery that it is to *relieve* him.

**Every recruit makes their settlement safe.** That is the open-world loop: the Ring stays as it is, the island is hostile everywhere else, and each Riser you recruit turns their home into somewhere the dead rise, where you can rest, trade and set out from again.

## The Pinned, chalk, straps and the rite

- **The Pinned** can be killed, but a killed Pinned is still in the ground and comes back. This is the in-world reason enemies respawn.
- To end one for good, a Riser has to raise it. This takes three steps, in the moments after it is defeated and before the darkness pulls it back under:
  1. **Chalk.** Throw chalk over the fallen body. The spirit inside, invisible until now, takes shape in the dust: you see who it was.
  2. **Strap.** Bind the spirit with a Riser's strap. This holds it in place against the pull of the dark. A strap does not hold forever; it buys time.
  3. **The Rite of Rising.** Say the rite before the strap gives. The spirit rises instead of being pinned again.
- The rite only works if the dead is willing to go. In play, the strap's hold is your timer and the rite is a conversation: the spirit half-remembers who it was, and you have to find out what is keeping it here. Stronger Pinned strain the strap harder, so better straps mean more time.
- **The dying.** The same tools work on a person at the moment of death. Anyone can throw chalk and tie a strap, which holds the spirit until a Riser arrives to say the rite. So chalk and straps are what ordinary people need most, they are scarce outside the Ring, and who gets them is politics.

## The Pilgrimage (main quest)

**Act I. Rhodes.** Learn the rite. Raise your first Pinned just outside the gate. Get the Register: eighty blank lines and no directions beyond what people tell you.

**Act II. The island.** The outer settlements do not want you. They remember the last pilgrims. They ask why Rhodes wants their dead, why straps cost what they cost, and why the Ring stayed shut for 134 years. They have their own death customs: some burn, some worship their Pinned ancestors, some hide. Recruits are found here, one at a time, and each one is a person with a reason to say no.

**Act III. Gornn.** The dragon tells you the truth, and it is mostly true: Wukhan is why the suffering continues. His answer is to go back and destroy the machine now. What he leaves out is that he is the one who broke it, and that he is sending you to finish what he could not. The last Pilgrimage took his advice and died at the Ring. If you take it too, the Ring drops with too few Risers to replace it, and the Under takes everything.

**Act IV. The Undying.** A half-machine people who are rebuilt every time they die. They live around the stone Lion, guard it, and are remade by the trace of life it still gives off. Here you learn where the Lion has been all along, and that the first 88 took its ember. They know more history than Rhodes does. Their offer: give them one Riser (you), and over 10,000 years they will replace you piece by piece until you are the new Wukhan. One person praying forever; everyone else saved. They are not villains. They are compassion reduced to arithmetic.

**Act V. The Ring closes.** You come home with the truth and the Houses reject it, because a world that does not need Wukhan does not need them either. You fight through your own civilisation, with however many Risers you brought.

**Act VI. The core.** Wukhan is the final arena, not the final villain.

### Endings

1. **Destroy him** (Gornn's). Without 88 Risers to take over, the Ring falls.
2. **Replace him** (the Undying's). You become the next machine. The world is saved and nothing is learned.
3. **Relieve him.** With 88 Risers holding the island, you shut Wukhan down and take the ember out. Wukhan's last words: *"The Ring is carried."* The Ring opens. The 88 go home and teach the rite to anyone who will learn it. People still die. Now every people can raise their own.

### The last choice: the ember

Relieving Wukhan leaves you holding the Lion's ember. What you do with it is the final decision of the game, and it is optional.

- **Bring the Lion back.** Carry the ember across the island and return it to the stone Lion. It wakes. It has no wings and never will again, so the Risers are still needed, but the world has its creator back. The cost: the Undying were living on the stone's leftover life, and a living Lion does not leak. They become mortal. And Gornn is still out there.
- **Leave it stone.** Divide the ember among the 88, a spark each. The Lion stays a statue and the world belongs to the people in it. The Risers' safe places hold for good, and the Undying go on as they are.

Ending 3 needs all 88. With fewer, shutting him down saves only as much of the island as your Risers can cover. The count is the difficulty slider.

**The 88th Riser.** You recruit 79. The last blank line in the Register is yours, and you are not offered it until the other 87 are filled. This keeps the club's own line ("we're looking for the 88th Riser") as the last beat of the game.

## Player freedom

The rule the game is built on: you can do anything, and the world answers. The Pilgrimage is offered, never required.

- **Nobody is protected.** Any character can be robbed, lied to, attacked or killed, including founders and possible recruits.
- **Murder has a built-in consequence.** Whoever you kill sinks like anyone else and comes back as one of the Pinned, unless somebody raises them. You can end up saying the rite over your own victim, and they remember you.
- **The 88 can be lost.** Each line in the Register belongs to one person. If that person dies unrecruited, the line stays blank and the full "relieve him" ending is gone for that playthrough. The game tells you, and carries on.
- **Crime is local.** Theft, grave-robbing and stealing straps or chalk are judged by whoever saw it and whoever they tell. Reputation travels by word of mouth between settlements, at the speed people travel.
- **You can ignore the story.** Trade straps, rise in a House, hunt the Pinned for pay, work for Gornn, live among the Undying, or never leave Rhodes. The island keeps going either way.

## NPC minds

- **Every NPC is an individual.** Each has a persona sheet (history, voice, values, what they want, what they are afraid of), a private memory of what the player and other NPCs have said and done to them, a disposition towards the player in the Morrowind sense, and a small set of drives that decide what they do when the player is not around. The persona and memory condition a shared language model; the per-NPC part is the state, which is theirs alone and persists.
- **Recruitment is persuasion, not a quest flag.** Each of the 80 has someone they lost who never rose. They join when that is resolved, and how you resolve it is open. There is no dialogue option labelled "recruit".
- **The Pinned are NPCs too.** A Pinned runs on a degraded copy of the person it was: gaps in memory, one fixation. Chalk shows you that person, and the rite is a conversation with them. A village's monster is somebody's father, and the village's NPCs remember him.
- **Directions, not markers.** NPCs tell you where things are from what they actually know, and they can be wrong, out of date, or lying.
- **Wukhan is the one scripted mind in the game.** Everyone else improvises. He has three lines and repeats them.
- **Founders write themselves.** Their sheets come from the real eight.
