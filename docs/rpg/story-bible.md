# 88 Risers — RPG story bible

An adaptation of the Body Tea / Wukong lore for an open-world RPG in the Morrowind mould: one hand-made island, no quest markers, factions you join, and NPCs who each run on their own AI personality.

## The premise in one paragraph

The dead cannot rise on their own any more. Anything that dies and touches the ground unattended sinks, rots and comes back wrong. A **Riser** is someone who takes a dying hand and holds it until it lets go, so the dead pass on instead of sinking. There are eight Risers left, in one village, inside one small ring of safe ground. You arrive as a pilgrim. Your pilgrimage is to bring the number to **88**, because 88 is exactly how many hands it takes to raise the thing that broke the world.

## Cosmology

Three layers: **the Still** above (nothingness, completion, rest), the living world in the middle, and **the Under** below (not punishment, just death that was never finished).

1. The Winged Lion governs the whole cycle. Life descends; the dead rise through its wings and pass to the Still. Nothing rots.
2. Gornn tears the wings off. The 88 pinions fall to earth. Life still comes down. Death no longer goes up.
3. Mankind is taught the Raise: a manual way to say "Lion, gather this one", one death at a time. The Old Hands collect the fallen pinions to do it.
4. The world outgrows them. Too many deaths, not enough hands. They build Wukong, cage all 88 pinions in his 88 arms, and stand up from the table. This is their sin: they outsourced the one job that was theirs.
5. **The Tragedy of the Boy.** A child asks Wukong: "If the Lion is God, why does it let this happen? Go and make it fix it." Wukong obeys, climbs the Stairway, and finds an empty throne, because the Lion is not up there. It is spread through every living thing, and 88 pieces of it are locked in his own arms. The Stairway gives way. Wukong falls.
6. **The Fall.** Eighty of his arms shatter on impact and their pinions scatter across the island. Eight arms survive. With them he bends his staff into the Ring and holds it. He has been holding it ever since, repeating the only order he still understands: *Protect the Ring.*
7. The Boy died in the fall, unraised. He was the first of the Pinned. He is still inside the wreck.

## Why 88, and what the power is for

Each loose pinion lodged in a person. Not a bloodline: a pinion finds whoever was holding a dying hand when it landed, and passes on the same way. Its bearer has **the Grip**, the ability to Raise. There are 88 pinions, so there can only ever be 88 Risers. Eight never left: their bearers' line became the founders of Rhodes.

Everyone in Rhodes believes the Pilgrimage exists to gather the 88 and plug them back into Wukong so the machine runs again. That is the lie the game is built to dismantle.

The truth: **Wukong is the oldest unraised dead thing in the world.** He died in the Fall and nobody held his hand. Everything since is one enormous Pinned creature refusing to let go. He has 88 arms. To raise him, every arm needs a hand, all at once. Eighty-seven is not enough; the grip slips and the Ring fails with him still in it.

So the 88 are not fuel. They are the minimum number of people needed to do for the machine the one thing the machine was built to do for everyone else.

The Ring is only as wide as eight Risers can hold. **Every recruit widens it.** That is the open-world unlock: no level gates, just safe ground growing outward from the village, and Rhodes growing with it.

## The Raise, straps and chalk

- **The Pinned** can be killed, but a killed Pinned is still in the ground and comes back. This is the in-world reason enemies respawn.
- To end one for good you **Raise** it: bring it to a table (any flat surface and a pinion will do), take its hand, and hold. You do not win by pinning it. You win when it lets go. In play this is a conversation under pressure: the dead thing still half-remembers who it was, and you have to find out what it is holding on to.
- **Straps.** When a grip slips in arm wrestling, the hands are strapped together. A Riser straps a dying person's hand so they do not go to ground before a table can be reached. Straps are scarce, and who gets one is politics.
- **Chalk.** What is left after a clean Raise. It is carried in a chalk bag, it marks safe ground, and it is the island's only honest currency.

## The Pilgrimage (main quest)

**Act I. Rhodes.** Learn the Raise. Raise your first Pinned just outside the gate. Get the Register: eighty blank lines and no directions beyond what people tell you.

**Act II. The island.** The outer settlements do not want you. They remember the last pilgrims. They ask why Rhodes wants their dead, why straps cost what they cost, and why the Ring stayed shut for 134 years. They have their own death customs: some burn, some worship their Pinned ancestors, some hide. Recruits are found here, one at a time, and each one is a person with a reason to say no.

**Act III. Gornn.** The dragon tells you the truth, and it is mostly true: Wukong is why the suffering continues. His answer is to go back and break the machine. The last Pilgrimage took that advice and died at the Ring. If you take it too, Wukong dies unraised, the Ring drops, and the Under takes everything.

**Act IV. The Unpinned.** A half-machine people who live beside the Lion's Egg and are rebuilt every time they die. They never need raising, so they see the problem as engineering. They know more history than Rhodes does. Their offer: give them one pinion-bearer (you), and over 10,000 years they will replace you piece by piece until you are the new Colossus. One person at the table forever; everyone else saved. They are not villains. They are compassion reduced to arithmetic.

**Act V. The Ring closes.** You come home with the truth and the Grips reject it, because accepting it ends their order. You fight through your own civilisation, with however many Risers you brought.

**Act VI. The core.** Wukong is the final arena, not the final villain.

### Endings

1. **The Pin** (Gornn's). Break the machine. The Ring falls.
2. **The Seat** (the Unpinned's, or Rhodes's own version of it). Plug the 88, or yourself, into the Colossus. The world is saved by people who never get to stand up.
3. **The Great Raise.** 88 hands on 88 arms. Hold until he lets go. The Boy passes first. Wukong's last words: *"The Ring is carried."* The Ring does not break; it opens, and its pieces become 88 small rings. Each Riser walks home with their pinion and sets a table in their own town, and teaches the Grip to anyone who has ever held a dying hand. People still die. Now every people can raise their own.

Ending 3 is only available with all 88. With fewer, the honest outcomes are 1, 2, or a partial Raise that saves some of the island and not the rest. The count is the difficulty slider.

**The 88th Riser.** You recruit 79. The last blank line in the Register is yours, and you are not offered it until the other 87 are seated. This keeps the club's own line ("we're looking for the 88th Riser") as the last beat of the game.

## NPC minds

The setting gives the AI a job to do rather than making it a feature bolted on.

- **Every NPC is an individual.** Each has a persona sheet (history, voice, values, what they want, what they are afraid of), a private memory of what the player and other NPCs have said and done to them, a disposition towards the player in the Morrowind sense, and a small set of drives that decide what they do when the player is not around. The persona and memory condition a shared language model; the per-NPC part is the state, which is theirs alone and persists.
- **Recruitment is persuasion, not a quest flag.** Each of the 80 has something they are still holding: someone they did not raise. They join when that is resolved, and how you resolve it is open. There is no dialogue option labelled "recruit".
- **The Pinned are NPCs too.** A Pinned creature runs on a degraded copy of the person it was: gaps in memory, one fixation. The Raise is a conversation with that. A village's monster is somebody's father, and the village's NPCs remember him.
- **Directions, not markers.** NPCs tell you where things are from what they actually know, and they can be wrong, out of date, or lying.
- **Wukong is the one scripted mind in the game.** Everyone else improvises. He has three lines and repeats them. In a world of people who can answer anything, the machine that cannot is the thing you feel sorry for.
- **Founders write themselves.** Their sheets come from the real eight.
